## Summary

A lesson on finding the minimum viable agreement (MVA), using the analogy of different frying pans and stoves. The narrative posits that successful, inclusive agreements are not based on the largest common elements, but on the smallest, essential shared foundation (like the flat bottom and heat from below) that allows the most diverse parties to connect.

## Concepts

- [[concepts/smallest-common-agreement|Smallest Common Agreement]]: The core principle that effective agreement is not based on the largest number of shared traits, but on the smallest, most fundamental shared element (e.g., the flat bottom and the heat source) that still allows for functionality and connection.
- [[concepts/scope-of-agreement|Scope of Agreement]]: The relationship between the size of an agreement and the number of participants who can adhere to it. A larger agreement limits participation, while a smaller, essential agreement maximizes inclusion.
- [[concepts/minimum-viable-agreement-mva|Minimum Viable Agreement (MVA)]]: The foundational, necessary set of conditions or principles required for a system or group to function, while maintaining maximum flexibility for external contributions.

## Entities

- [[entities/the-book|The Book]]: A mysterious, untitled book read by Лиля, containing analogies and principles regarding agreement, found in her grandmother's closet.
- [[entities/frying-pan-and-stove-analogy|Frying Pan and Stove Analogy]]: A continuous metaphor used to teach the concept of common ground: the variation in pans and stoves represents individual differences, while the 'flat bottom' and 'heat from below' represent the necessary common agreement.
- [[entities/the-teacher|Дед (The Teacher)]]: The guiding figure who leads the lesson, presenting the philosophical concept of consensus and shared principles to the class.

## Claims

- The whole agreement is the flat bottom and the heat from below. [source:lesson-2-the-book-on-the-desk-d5f66c30]
- The smallest agreement that still works is the one that lets the most strangers meet. [source:lesson-2-the-book-on-the-desk-d5f66c30]
- If the agreement is too big, only identical pans fit. [source:lesson-2-the-book-on-the-desk-d5f66c30]
